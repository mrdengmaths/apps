What is the Natural Logarithm?

The Base $e$

Just like base 10 is common in science, higher mathematics loves the special number Euler's number ($e$).

$$ e \approx 2.71828... $$

It is the unique base where the derivative of the exponential function is itself!

The Notation: $\ln(x)$

A logarithm with base $e$ is so important it gets its own special notation. Instead of writing $\log_e(x)$, we write $\ln(x)$ (pronounced "ell-en of x").

$$ \ln(x) \equiv \log_e(x) $$

Key Takeaway

Whenever you see $\ln$, just remember it is exactly the same as any other log you've learned, it simply has a base of $e$.

Natural Log Rules

Because $\ln$ is simply $\log_e$, all your standard logarithm laws apply perfectly. Notice how $\ln$ and $\log_e$ are completely interchangeable!

Product Rule

$$ \ln(ab) = \ln(a) + \ln(b) $$
Same as: $$ \log_e(ab) = \log_e(a) + \log_e(b) $$

Quotient Rule

$$ \ln\left(\frac{a}{b}\right) = \ln(a) - \ln(b) $$
Same as: $$ \log_e\left(\frac{a}{b}\right) = \log_e(a) - \log_e(b) $$

Power Rule

$$ \ln(a^n) = n\ln(a) $$
Same as: $$ \log_e(a^n) = n\log_e(a) $$

Special Values

$$ \ln(1) = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad \ln(e) = 1 $$
Same as: $$ \log_e(1) = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad \log_e(e) = 1 $$

Cancellation Properties (Inverse)

Because exponential and log functions undo each other:

$$ \ln(e^x) = x $$
$$ e^{\ln(x)} = x $$

Exponential & Log Graphs

The functions $y = e^x$ and $y = \ln(x)$ are inverse functions.

Inverse Property

Because they undo each other, their graphs are exact reflections across the diagonal line $y = x$.

Every point $(a, b)$ on $y=e^x$ becomes $(b, a)$ on $y=\ln(x)$.

$y = e^x$
$y = x$ (Mirror Line)
